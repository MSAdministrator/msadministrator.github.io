Introduction

Before I dive in, I just wanted to first thank you for your interest in me; it does truly mean a lot to me. This post was also featured in The Thor Collective’s Dispatch. Now, lets switch gears.

If you are curious, the past two years I have been working as a Senior Software Engineer focused on learning new languages, frameworks, architecture patterns, scaling services, metrics, advanced CI/CD and even GCP itself. These were all new to me and most of my time over the past two years has been focusing on both learning and implementing these in a mirad of ways.

Some Context

Okay, before I go to far I want to share some context here. I won’t go into any great details regarding my experience but will outline my past roles with minor points. I believe this will set the stage for why I’m even writing this blog in the first place.

First of all, I didn’t go to college right after high school. I went full time in the evenings starting at 28 and got a BS in Science (Computer Information Systems). This was when I truly strarted my career in IT and eventually leading to information security as my passion.

  • [1 year] Helpdesk - was support at a small university after competing my internship there - total of a year or so
  • [1.5 years] System Support Analyst / System Administrator - Focused on AD & GPO and Windows image management (MDT, WDS, SCCM, etc.) for thousands of systems
  • [3 years] Security Analyst - Focused on DFIR but built tools using PowerShell / VB.net / C#. Worked on phishing defense, vulnerability management, group policy/ ad security / etc.
  • [1 year] Solution Engineer - Recruited to PhishMe (now Cofense) because of open-source report button & helped build the “PhishMe Reporter” product to 18 Million installs globally
  • [2 years] Manager / Technical Product Owner/Manager - While at Cofense I managed a team of 7 solutions engineers and 3 software engineers where we defined, designed, implemented & supported global customers
  • [4 years] Security Research Engineer / Security Solutions Architect - Created content and webinars around SOAR and automation. I also worked to defined & built SOAR automation systems (large workflows) used by some of the largest companies on the globe. I also released lots of open source security tools.
  • [3 months~] Senior Detection Validation Engineer - Worked briefly on detection validation but also had to learn Ruby and Rails while doing so. RIF occured.

Again these are all high level details but I think it sets the stage and my point. I’ve always been hands on keyboard but never a full time software engineer. All of the roles above required me to work with people. I’ve never said I had all the answers, and to me working with people and having a conversation helps me think. Honestly it may be weird but I think much better with people than on my own. My ADHD seems to include a million different thoughts at once so having a conversation allows me to focus on one of those things.

Anyways, that’s not the point of this post.

What have I learned?

I knew from the beginning that being a software engineer would be a different role than I’ve experienced before but I still wanted to try; and overall I’m glad I did. For the majority of the past two years I have been learning and using Golang which is quite a bit different than Python. I’ve been using Go to build, augment, etc. microservices as part of an event-driven architecture. Obviously I cannot share any details here but I can tell you that building a robust CI/CD infrastructure is paramount.

Start

In retrospect, I have realized that creating software for software sake does not motive me and really just adds way more stress and anxiety than I want in my life. I enjoy collaborating (communicating) with other engineers, analysts, researchers and technicians to help solve problems they have. This also allows me to have immediate impact (and feedback) which further motivates me. I like helping where I can.

After the past two years I’ve done some reflection on what I want for my career. Here’s a short list if you’re interested:

  • I thrive in a team environment where verbal and written communication is second nature.

  • Ideation, brainstorming and general collaboration to solve problems motives me. I’ve found many times where I’ve had conversations with engineers for hours and those were the most productive for me. I don’t know all the answers, but I know what I don’t know.

  • I like giving back to the infosec community via open-source tools. Over the past few years I’ve missed this – learning new languages, tools, frameworks, etc. doesn’t leave much room for anything else.

  • Golang - I had never used Golang and over the past two years I have spent most of my time using it to build microservices

  • gRPC - Google’s RPC (Remote Procedure Call) framework (open-source) for high-speed network communications (HTTP2) between services

  • Protobuf - A cross platform and cross language framework for serialization/deserialization of messages over gRPC
